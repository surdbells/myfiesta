Help

I bought a ticket and nothing arrived

Tickets are emailed within a minute of payment clearing. If it is not there, check the spam folder for the address you typed at checkout — a mistyped address is the usual cause, and we cannot see what was typed. Get in touch with your order reference and we will resend them.

How do I get into the event?

Open the link in your confirmation email and show the QR code at the door. Turn your screen brightness up — a dim screen is the most common reason a scanner will not read a ticket. If the camera will not focus, the code printed under it can be read out instead.

My ticket says it admits more than one person

Some tickets — a couple's ticket, a table — let in several people on one code. You do not all have to arrive together: the door can let part of the group in and the rest stay on the ticket for later.

Can I get a refund?

Refunds are the organizer's decision, not ours — they set the terms for their own event. Contact them first. If an event is cancelled outright, the organizer refunds through us and the money returns the way it came, which takes a few working days depending on your bank. The refund page has the details.

Can I give my ticket to someone else?

Yes. Forward them the link from your confirmation email — whoever shows the QR at the door gets in. If the event requires ID matching the name on the ticket, the organizer will say so on the event page.

I did not get a reminder

Reminders can be turned off from the link at the bottom of any of them. Turning them off never affects your tickets or your order emails.

Terms

Not yet reviewed by a lawyer. This describes how the platform actually works and is accurate about the mechanics, but it must be reviewed against Canadian and Nigerian consumer law before myFiesta takes real money.

What myFiesta does

myFiesta sells tickets on behalf of organizers. The contract for the event itself is between you and the organizer named on the event page. We handle the sale, the ticket and the payment; they run the night.

Buying

A ticket is yours once payment clears. Prices are shown in the organizer's currency, and tax is shown separately at checkout unless the price already includes it, in which case it says so. Stock is held while you are on the payment page and released if you do not finish.

Refunds and cancellations

Organizers decide refunds for their own events, and a refund is made ticket by ticket, to the original payment method. When a ticket is refunded, its share of our service fee is refunded with it. If an organizer cancels an event, they are responsible for refunding it. If a payment arrives after the last ticket has sold, no ticket is issued and the whole payment is returned automatically. The refund page sets out exactly how each case works.

Selling

Organizers are responsible for the event being what they said it was, for holding the licences the venue and the law require, and for the accuracy of what appears on their event page. Payouts are made after the event, less any refunds. Our fee is a service charge shown to the buyer at checkout and added to what they pay — it does not come out of the ticket price you set.

Behaviour we will not host

Events that are fraudulent, that sell something illegal, or that exist to launder money. We will withhold a payout and cancel an event where we have reason to believe any of those apply.

Privacy

Not yet reviewed by a lawyer. The description below is accurate about what the system does — it is written from the same map the export and deletion jobs run from — but it needs review against PIPEDA and the NDPA before launch.

What we hold

If you buy a ticket: your name, email address, and phone number if you gave one; what you bought and what you paid; and the ticket itself, including whether it was scanned at the door. We never see or store your card number — that goes directly to Stripe or Paystack.

If you organize events: the above, plus your organization's details and the account you sign in with.

When you sign up or buy, that you accepted the terms, this privacy policy and the refund policy: which version of them, and when. Your IP address and browser are not kept with it.

Why

To sell you a ticket, to get it to you, to let a door confirm it, and to pay the organizer. Your email address is also used to remind you about events you hold tickets to, which you can turn off from any reminder without affecting your tickets.

Who else sees it

The organizer of an event you bought a ticket to sees your name, email and what you bought — they need it to run their guest list. Our payment processors see what they need to take the payment. Our email provider sees the address a message is sent to. Nobody else, and we do not sell anything to anybody.

Your data, and removing it

Ask for a copy of everything held about you, or ask to have it removed, from here. A link goes to that address first — it is how we know the request is yours.

Financial records are kept, with your identity stripped out of them, because we are required to be able to account for money that moved. One thing survives deletion deliberately: if you have asked us to stop emailing you, we keep a record of that address so we do not start again the next time you buy a ticket. An export leaves out ticket codes — those open doors, and your tickets are always in the link from your confirmation email.

Where it lives

On servers we run, with backups. Some of the services above hold copies outside Canada and Nigeria as part of doing their job.

Refunds

Not yet reviewed by a lawyer. This describes how refunds work on the platform today and is accurate about the mechanics, but it must be reviewed against Canadian and Nigerian consumer law before myFiesta takes real money.

Who decides

The organizer named on the event page. It is their event and their money, so they decide whether a ticket is refunded, and they issue the refund through us. If you want one, ask them first. myFiesta refunds on its own only in the one case below where no ticket was ever issued.

What comes back

A refund is made ticket by ticket, never as a part-amount. For each ticket refunded you get back its full share of what you paid: its price after any discount, its share of any tax, and its share of our service fee. The service fee is not kept back.

A refunded ticket stops working at the door straight away. A ticket that has already been scanned can still be refunded if the organizer chooses to. A free ticket has nothing to refund.

Where the money goes

Back to the card or account you paid with — through Stripe for Canadian dollars and Paystack for naira. It cannot be sent anywhere else. Once it is issued, the time it takes to appear depends on your bank or card issuer, usually a few working days. If the payment processor turns a refund down, your tickets stay valid until it goes through.

If an event is cancelled

Everybody holding a ticket is emailed, even if they have turned off other emails. Cancelling an event offers the organizer the choice to refund every paid order at the same moment, and that is the default. An organizer who cancels without refunding is still responsible for refunding under our terms — contact them, or us, with your order reference.

If the tickets sold out while you were paying

If your payment arrives after the last ticket has gone, no ticket is issued and the whole payment, service fee included, is sent back automatically. You get an email saying so.

Giving a ticket back

Some organizers let you give a ticket back from the link in your confirmation email, up to a cut-off before the doors that they set. The ticket link offers it only where the organizer allows it. The ticket stops working as soon as you give it back. When somebody else buys that place, you are refunded what you paid for it, service fee included. If nobody does, there is no refund — and until somebody does, you can take the ticket back.

Asking about a refund

The organizer first, for anything about the event. For anything about a payment, or if you cannot reach them, write to us with the order reference from your confirmation email.
